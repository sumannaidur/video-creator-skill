# Creative direction: How does the human heart pump blood? (te-IN, Paper Explainer, full-bleed)

## Reference targets (P018.1 audit: Painted Story, Paper Collage, Flat Science)

- Full-bleed paper scenes that each have a large, characterful hero. No empty frames.
- One warm key light from the upper left, crisp cut-paper shadows, paper and gouache grain.
- Short word captions, plus a hook headline on the first frame.
- Callouts that use narration words only, with a small "simplified · not to scale" note.

## Visual idea

The viewer travels with a few blood cells through one consistent paper heart.

- A person at dawn has a glowing paper window over the chest, where the heart beats. The camera pushes through the window.
- The same heart (exterior, then cut open) is the stage for the whole loop: body → right side → lungs → left side → body.
- The valves are shown as paper doors.
- The ending pulls back from the heart's spark to the person.

## Anatomy rules (guardrails)

- **Frontal view:** the heart's right side is on the viewer's left. This is stated in the plan, not the narration.
- **Chamber layout:** atria above, ventricles below. The left ventricle wall is drawn thicker than the right (standard anatomy; visual only, not narrated).
- **Valves:**
  - tricuspid between the right atrium and right ventricle;
  - mitral between the left atrium and left ventricle;
  - pulmonary valve at the root of the pulmonary trunk;
  - aortic valve at the root of the aorta.
- **Vessels:**
  - superior and inferior vena cava into the right atrium;
  - pulmonary trunk from the right ventricle, splitting towards both lungs;
  - pulmonary veins into the left atrium;
  - aorta from the left ventricle, arching over with three branches.
- **Colour:** blue and red blood is a teaching convention, and the on-screen note says so.
- **Lungs:** the right lung has three lobes, the left has two and a cardiac notch.
- **No numbers** except the narrated 60–100 per minute.
- **SA node** is placed high in the right atrium, near the superior vena cava.

## Shots (one per narration segment)

| Shot | Purpose |
|---|---|
| pulse (s1) | Hook. A person on a rooftop at sunrise; an ECG line draws across the sky; the heart beats in a chest window; a "60–100 / min" tag appears. Push in. |
| heart (s2) | The exterior paper heart turns; the cross-section opens on "four"; atria and ventricles are tagged. |
| right (s3) | Blue cells enter through the vena cavae into the right atrium, then the right ventricle, then up the pulmonary trunk. The camera follows upward. |
| lungs (s4) | Branching paper lungs; a magnified air-sac circle where blue cells turn red; red cells return to the left atrium and drop into the left ventricle. |
| body (s5) | The left ventricle squeezes and red cells shoot up the aorta. A full-body paper silhouette, with arteries drawing to the head and then the toes. |
| valves (s6) | A close-up of a vessel: two paper door leaflets swing open one way, and backflow is blocked. |
| lubdub (s7) | The heart again: valves snap shut with cut-paper "లబ్" and "డబ్" letters and sound rings. |
| spark (s8) | A spark at the SA node; an electrical wave sweeps from the atria to the ventricles; pull back to the person. |

## Motion

- The heart beats in a lub-dub double pulse wherever it is visible.
- Cells travel at constant speed along the anatomical paths.
- The camera travels in every shot.
- Transitions use dissolves on matched subjects: chest window to heart, heart to lungs, lungs to heart.
