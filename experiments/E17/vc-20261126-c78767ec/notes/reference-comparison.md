# Reference-quality comparison (E17, heart, te-IN)

**Benchmark:** the Viral Styles Launchpad previews as observed in P018.1: Flat Science "coffee/adenosine" and "bees", Painted Story "Venice", and the style thumbnails. Stills only; the reference's motion was not assessed.

**This is the agent's own assessment, not an owner review.** Premium quality is not claimed.

## Artistic review gate (before Full HD)

- **Keyframes v1** (`render/keys-v1/review-sheet.png`): judged **substantially below** the reference.
  - The backgrounds were plain paper.
  - The body and valve frames were sparse.
  - The cells were too small to read.
  - Too much of it read as a textbook diagram.
- **One corrective pass (keyframes v2):**
  - in-body environments: deep tissue tones, glowing depth, layered tissue folds, drifting out-of-focus cells;
  - cells 50% larger;
  - fuller body and valve compositions.
- **Keyframes v3:** fixes for the body framing, near-camera cells drifting over the heart, and a closer lub-dub framing.
- **Decision:** still below the reference in finish, but no longer substantially below in richness. A Full HD render costs about 10 minutes, so it was rendered and the remaining gap is recorded here.

## Where it now matches the reference characteristics

- **Full-bleed frames:** short word-synchronised captions with an active-word marker, a hook headline with a highlighted keyword on the first frame, and narration-word callouts.
- **No empty frames:** every shot has a layered environment (rooftop city at sunrise, in-body tissue spaces, the lung cavity) with depth cues.
- **One consistent hero:** the paper heart, exterior and cross-section, carried through the whole loop.
- **Recognizable anatomy built from components:**
  - great vessels;
  - coronary vessels and fat on the exterior;
  - valve leaflets with chordae and papillary muscles;
  - a thicker left-ventricle wall;
  - lungs with three and two lobes and a cardiac notch, plus a bronchial tree;
  - the SA node.
- **A character opening** with an in-scene ECG line and a chest-window push. There is camera travel in every shot, and the heart beats in a lub-dub double pulse throughout.

## Where it remains below the reference

- **Illustration finish:** the reference's vector work has softer volumetric shading and glow (the Flat Science brain, receptors and bee). Ours is cut-paper geometry with uniform outlines. The heart cross-section still reads partly as a classroom diagram.
- **Variety:** the heart cross-section carries four of eight shots (heart, right side, lub-dub, spark). Camera framing differs, but the compositions are similar.
- **Character:** the opening person is a stiff paper rig: one moving arm, head tilt and blinks. The reference uses characterful heroes, such as the bee.
- **Weaker shots:**
  - The body and valve shots are serviceable but plain.
  - The lungs' pulmonary vessels are straight tubes.
- **Hook:** the opening has weaker visual punch than the reference's large hero subjects. The person is mid-size and the heart is a small window.
- **Motion:** cells are small discs on linear paths. There is no fluid or volumetric flow.

## Accuracy notes

- Every narrated fact traces to NHLBI pages (`research/claims.json`).
- Three candidate facts were dropped because the sources did not state them: fist size, beats per day, and the thickest wall.
- Blue/red blood is marked on screen as a teaching convention.
- The anatomy is simplified and frontal, with the heart's right side on the viewer's left. **It needs expert review.**
