# P018.1 Reference-Driven Premium Quality Audit

**Status:** analysis and plan only. No code, installs, renders or commits.
**Date:** 2026-11-26.

**Reference:** the owner's benchmark at https://theaitoolfinds.gumroad.com/l/viral-styles-launchpad/. The landing content is embedded from `/l/wbsqwp/landing/embed`.

## 1. What was actually observed

The reference **was accessible**. Everything below comes from viewing it in the browser. Nothing was downloaded.

**Evidence base:**
- **12 style thumbnails:** JPEG stills. Three are 360×640 and nine are 216×384.
- **4 preview videos** (720×1280). I sampled 5 frames from each:
  - flat-science "coffee / adenosine";
  - flat-science "how bees make honey";
  - painted-story "Venice sinking";
  - the page's "free prompt" comparison clip.
- **Product claims** (seller's words, not verified):
  - "12 styles", "9 for full stories, 3 for intros".
  - Word-synced captions, 1080×1920.
  - "~20 min to a finished video".
  - Free prompt "looks like an infographic" versus "big animated visuals".

**Not observable (not inferred):**
- Motion curves, easing and transition behaviour. I saw frames, not continuous motion.
- Audio, voice and music.
- The production method or toolchain.
- Indic-language rendering: all reference text is English.
- Seven of the twelve styles were seen only as one small thumbnail each.

### Observed reference characteristics

| Aspect | Observation |
|---|---|
| Frame | **Full-bleed 9:16.** There is no card border and no separate caption panel. The illustration fills the whole frame. |
| Captions | Short **word-synced chunks** of 1–3 words, in a dark pill mid-lower frame. One keyword is coloured (e.g. "called **adenosine**", "hits all at **once**"). Painted Story uses a torn-paper serif caption instead. |
| Hook | Frame 1 has a large bold headline with one keyword in a coloured box: "Inside your **espresso** machine", "Dive to the ocean's **deepest** point". |
| Hero | One characterful subject fills 40–60% of the frame: brain, bee, sub, earbuds, espresso machine, Sun. |
| Depth and atmosphere | Every frame has a layered background: bokeh particles, glowing neuron networks, a starfield or nebula, honeycomb texture, sky, water and strata. **No frame was empty.** |
| Lighting | Strong glows and bloom: emissive bonds, a glowing honey cell, the sub's headlight beam, X-ray internals. There are rim lights, soft shadows and vignettes. |
| Materials | Paper-grain and gouache texture (Painted Story); torn paper, stamps and grid paper (Paper Collage). There are two-tone shaded flat shapes with highlights, translucent wings, a fuzzy bee body, PBR-looking 3D, a translucent blue X-ray shell, and a granulated Sun surface. |
| Callouts | A consistent system: a small dark rounded label, with a bold title plus a monospace subtitle. A thin leader line ends in a dot. A tiny mono footnote reads "simplified · not to scale". |
| Data UI in-scene | Progress bars ("water 29%"), counters ("1/12"), depth scales (10,900 m) and big numerals ("13,690 km", "109 Earths"). |
| Story | Scene changes follow the narration: question, then mechanism, then payoff. The camera changes scale between shots: a whole brain, receptors, then the brain again. |
| "Free prompt" baseline | Dark purple. Three icons in circles, pills and small text, built up step by step. The seller presents this as the *inferior* look. |

## 2. Our outputs compared (E11, E13, E15)

**Cross-cutting gaps** (they apply to every style and have the largest visual effect):

1. **Slide framing.**
   - Every style renders a rounded **card plus a separate caption panel**. The panel holds full sentences and takes about 20–30% of the frame.
   - The reference is full-bleed, with short overlaid word-chunk captions.
   - This alone makes our frames read as "slides".
2. **Empty backgrounds.**
   - Our backgrounds are flat gradients or a blank field: whiteboard, tech, data, kinetic, X-ray and 3D.
   - The reference has no empty frames.
3. **Small heroes.**
   - The subject often fills only 15–25% of the frame: the whiteboard pot, the X-ray gearbox, the tech phone.
4. **No light.**
   - We lack glow, bloom, rim light and atmospheric haze.
   - Shadows are only drop shadows.
5. **No material texture.**
   - We have no grain, paper fibre, paint or surface detail.
   - E13's paper style is the best of ours and still clean vector.
6. **Weak hook.**
   - We have no headline-with-keyword opening frame.
7. **Blank transition frames.**
   - Mid-transition frames appear as pure black or flat stripes in our sheets: data `bars`, kinetic `shutter`, documentary `dip`, X-ray `sweep`, and the 3D `dolly` darkness.
   - None appeared in the reference samples. The sample is small, so this is not conclusive.
8. **Inconsistent callouts.**
   - Label styles differ per style and have no leader-line or subtitle convention.

**What already matches or exceeds the reference:**
- Cue-synced reveals.
- Correct Indic shaping in five locales.
- Native numerals.
- Deterministic preflight.
- Real meshing mechanics.
- E13's environmental storytelling (the character, train and workshop), which is the closest to the reference's richness.

## 3. Production techniques evaluated

The constraint is the existing hardware (i5-1145G7, CPU only) and no mandatory paid services. Where a cost is marked "unmeasured", it needs a bounded test before adoption.

| Technique | Verdict | Evidence and reasoning |
|---|---|---|
| **Full-bleed layout and word-chunk captions** | **Adopt first** | Pure layout, already inside the stack. Word timing exists (Mithi native word timing, `captions.py`). It closes the biggest observed gap for all nine styles. |
| **Advanced SVG and CSS** (gradient meshes by layering, masks, `mix-blend-mode: screen` glows, `feTurbulence` / `feDisplacementMap` grain and torn edges) | **Adopt** | Already our renderer, zero cost. Risk: per-frame SVG filters on CPU at 1080×1920 can be slow (unmeasured). Mitigation: bake static textures once per shot to a raster in the page, and animate only transforms and opacity. |
| **Layered imagery and parallax** (separate depth planes, particles, bokeh, haze) | **Adopt** | Directly reproduces the reference's depth and atmosphere, deterministic. Effort lives in the art, not infrastructure. |
| **Procedural textures** (paper fibre, gouache grain, honeycomb, starfield, strata noise) | **Adopt** | Seed-deterministic, no licensing exposure. Highest value for paper-explainer and flat-science. |
| **3D upgrade: bevels, environment light, bloom, anti-aliasing** | **Adopt in k3d, then measure** | Our renderer exists and is measured at 0.64 s per frame. Bloom and 2× supersampling add GPU-style work on SwiftShader. Cost is unmeasured; it may be 2–4×, and must be checked on one keyframe. |
| **three.js** (MIT; PBR, transmission, glTF import, post-processing) | **Spike only, ADR before adoption** | It would supply a translucent X-ray shell, glTF models and bloom faster than extending k3d. It is a new dependency, and SwiftShader performance is unmeasured. Decide on a one-keyframe side-by-side, not by assumption. |
| **Licensed CC0 asset libraries** (e.g. Poly Haven, ambientCG, Kenney: models, HDRIs, textures) | **Candidate, per-asset review** | Could raise 3D and documentary quality quickly. Licences need verifying per asset at the time of download, and every download needs owner approval. Not researched further here, by scope. |
| **Real licensed photos and footage for documentary** | **Required for that style** | Synthetic placeholders cannot reach the reference's richness. The style's gap is sourcing, not rendering. |
| **Local image generation** (SD/SDXL/FLUX-class) | **Defer** | There is no GPU; CPU generation time is unmeasured but expected to be slow. Model licences vary. Outputs are not independently animatable layers and cannot render Indic text. Revisit only for static painted backplates, with a bounded spike and a licence review. |

**SVG alone is not sufficient.** The reference's 3D, X-ray and scale styles depend on real lighting and translucency. **New tools are also not the first lever.** Most of the observed gap (framing, density, light, texture, hook, captions) can be closed inside the current stack.

## 4. Prioritised improvement plan

Order: visual impact first, then feasibility, then effort. Each line is a candidate atomic task. Effort: S = 1 task, M = 2–3 tasks, L = 4 or more tasks.

| # | Task | Styles | Impact | Feasibility | Effort |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|

| 4 | **Hero-scale rule** in creative direction and self-review: the hero is 40–60% of the frame, one subject per shot, and no frame without a background layer. **Recommended sequence:** #1, #2, #3 and #4 (shared core, all styles), then #5 and #6 (the most-used 2D styles), then #7 and #8 (3D track), then the rest.

**Proposed acceptance check for each task:**
- A side-by-side keyframe sheet against the reference observations above, using the 10-question self-review in `references/creative-direction.md`.
- Owner artistic sign-off.

## 5. Limits of this audit

- The reference was judged from **stills only**: 20 sampled video frames and 12 small thumbnails. Its motion quality, timing and audio were not assessed.
- Seller claims, including view counts and "~20 min", are reported as claims only.
- No render-cost figures are given for the proposed techniques. Each one needs one bounded measurement.
- Our outputs were compared using their existing contact sheets: E11, E13 final frames, and the E15 P017.1–P017.8 sheets.
- Human gates remain open: artistic acceptance, native-language review, licensing and publication.
