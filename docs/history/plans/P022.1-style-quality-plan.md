# P022.1 Style-quality improvement plan (nine existing styles)

**Status:** research only. Owner review is required before any implementation.
- No installs, downloads or production changes were made, and the released v0.1.0-beta is untouched.
- No new styles are proposed, and no single technology is assumed to fit every style.

**Date:** 2026-11-27.

## Evidence and its limits

- **Our output:** I inspected our own renders:
  - P018.6 full-bleed contact sheets for all nine styles (`experiments/E16/p018.6-all-styles/overview.png`);
  - the Paper construction keys (P018.4);
  - the E18 Tech keyframes;
  - the E19 Blender test frames (`experiments/E19/out/test`).
- **References:** researched through published process accounts and secondary analyses (linked below).
  - I did **not** play reference videos frame by frame in this task.
  - Descriptions of a reference's look combine those sources with general knowledge, labelled *(general knowledge)* where no source confirms them.
  - The owner should view the named references before artistic criteria are finalised.
- **Measured technology facts reused from P022 and E19:**
  - Blender 5.2 EEVEE on Iris Xe: about 5.3 s per steady frame at 540×960 and 32 samples, about 10 s initialisation, peak about 1.85 GiB RAM.
  - Repeatability between runs: SSIM 0.999998.
  - Our SVG/Chromium path: about 0.22 s per Full HD frame. This is estimated from E18 frame timestamps, not separately benchmarked.

## Cross-style diagnosis (from our renders)

These gaps appear in every style and are creative rather than technological:

1. **Small subjects in large empty frames.** The full-bleed demos place one small object in about 60–80% empty space (Whiteboard pot, X-Ray gear box, 3D box). Professional references fill the frame with one dominant subject plus a supporting environment.
2. **One shot size per scene.** There is little variation between wide, medium and close shots, no foreground framing, and little use of thirds or diagonals.
3. **Mechanical motion.** Linear or simple eased tweens, without anticipation, overshoot, follow-through, secondary motion or staggered settling.
4. **Weak transitions.** Cuts and blurs rather than match cuts, morphs or a continuous camera.
5. **Labels that don't match the style.** Small boxed tags appear in every style, whereas each style's references integrate type into the art (handwriting, annotation lines, kinetic type).

## How the criteria are meant (owner direction)

The criteria below are **context-sensitive quality principles, not quotas**:
- frame occupancy;
- camera movement;
- shadows and lighting;
- character acting;
- morphing;
- ambient motion.

Each is used when it serves the story and is omitted when it doesn't. A frame can be deliberately sparse, still or flat when the beat calls for it.

The only hard requirements are:
- technical gates: sync, legibility floor, safe areas, Indic shaping;
- accuracy, provenance and licensing gates.

Reviewers judge whether each choice serves the narration, not whether an effect is present.

## Per-style findings

Each style lists three or more references, its defining characteristics, our gaps, techniques and technology, acceptance criteria, and the smallest experiment.

### 1. Paper Explainer (cut paper)
- **References:**
  - TED-Ed lessons made with cut-out animation, which their "Making a TED-Ed Lesson" series describes as an old physical technique that software emulates ([transcript](https://interactivetranscript.chickenkiller.com/tao-video-ted-ed-hoat-hinh-hoa-zombie));
  - NFB cut-out tradition ([NFB](https://blog.nfb.ca/blog/2018/06/14/animation-techniques-cutout/));
  - papercraft explainers ([Make](https://makezine.com/article/craft/animated-papercraft-explanations)).
- **Defining traits:**
  - Physical layers with real cast shadows and paper grain and fibre.
  - Visible thickness at cut edges.
  - Hinged parts with replacement mouths and hands.
  - Motion stepped "on twos" with tiny placement jitter, so it reads as handmade *(general knowledge)*.
- **Our gaps:** P018.4 (the canal lock) is the strongest demo. It has textures, a figure rig and environments. It lacks:
  - drop shadows offset by layer depth;
  - edge thickness;
  - stepped timing;
  - acting: the figure's poses are stiff and there is no secondary motion on hair or clothes;
  - a camera that moves through layer depth (parallax).
- **Techniques and technology:** stay in SVG/Canvas.
  - A shadow and edge pass per layer depth.
  - Stepped-time sampling as a kit option (12 fps poses inside 30 fps).
  - Deterministic jitter per layer.
  - Parallax camera across depth layers.
  - Hinge rig with follow-through.
  - No new dependencies.
- **Quality principles:**
  - Cut layers read as physical paper: shadows and edges agree with their depth wherever layers overlap.
  - Paper texture is visible at phone size.
  - Parallax is used when the camera move clarifies space or follows the subject.
  - Stepped timing is used where the handmade feel supports the tone.
  - A character acts when a beat is about people or an action. A character is never added only for decoration.
- **Smallest experiment:** re-render 4 s of the P018.4 lock shot with shadows, stepped timing and parallax. Compare before and after at 540×960.

### 2. Tech Explainer (dark product studio)
- **References:**
  - Apple-style product reveals: hero reveal, then detail close-ups, then context; backlights lined up with edges; reflections that travel with motion ([Manfrotto](https://www.manfrotto.com/global-en/stories/how-to-shoot-cinematic-tech-videos-like-apple/)).
  - Apple TV's 2025 intro, shot with real glass and a moving camera ([9to5Mac](https://9to5mac.com/2025/11/06/apple-tvs-colorful-new-intro-was-created-with-real-glass-and-practical-effects/)).
  - Primer's programmatic Blender pipeline ([primerpython](https://github.com/Helpsypoo/primerpython)).
- **Defining traits:**
  - Dark sets.
  - Edge-lit materials (metal, glass) with moving reflections.
  - Shallow depth of field.
  - A slow, deliberate camera.
  - One hero object at a time.
- **Our gaps (E18):**
  - Flat vector phone and iso-block cities.
  - Lighting is faked with gradients.
  - Reflections don't move with the camera.
- **Techniques and technology:** this is the one style where real light and materials clearly pay off.
  - **E19 (in progress):** a Blender EEVEE hero shot with procedural materials. It measured practical frame times and achieved bevelled metal and glass, bokeh, rack focus, volumetric light and a capsule with anticipation and follow-through.
  - Keep SVG for UI, diagrams and type.
  - Composite captions with the existing `presentation.js`, as proved in E19.
- **Quality principles:**
  - Light is motivated and directs attention to the subject the narration is about.
  - Materials read as real only where that helps the viewer recognise a device.
  - Depth of field and camera moves are used when they clarify the subject or a change of subject.
  - UI and Indic text stay crisp and legible.
- **Smallest experiment:** finish E19 (preview, Full HD keyframes, matched comparison). The preview render was running at the time of writing (100 of 135 frames done).

### 3. Whiteboard
- **References:**
  - RSA Animate by Cognitive (Andrew Park), filmed over the illustrator's shoulder on one continuous board ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Whiteboard_animation), [Cognitive process](https://cognitive-media.squarespace.com/blog/the-whiteboard-animation-process-taking-your-message-from-idea-to-explainer-video));
  - Cognitive's "9 techniques" ([Cognitive](https://cognitive-media.squarespace.com/blog/what-is-whiteboard-animation-the-9-techniques-that-make-it-work));
  - Minute Physics' hand-drawn explainers *(general knowledge)*.
- **Defining traits:**
  - Dense visual note-taking on **one continuous canvas**, revealed by the camera.
  - Strokes drawn in the order a hand would draw them, with varying pressure.
  - Handwritten keywords as the main typography.
  - Witty visual puns.
  - Spot colour only.
- **Our gaps:**
  - Sparse doodles in a mostly empty frame.
  - No continuous canvas or camera trail.
  - Typeset labels in boxes instead of handwriting.
  - Strokes have uniform width.
- **Techniques and technology:** SVG stroke reveal (already present), with these changes:
  - Variable-width brush strokes, as SVG outline paths.
  - Path order and timing taken from the narration.
  - A world canvas larger than the frame, with the camera panning between drawings.
  - Handwriting-style lettering of narration keywords, using an open-licensed handwriting font for Latin and Indic scripts (licence to be verified per font). No new dependencies beyond the font check.
- **Quality principles:**
  - The board builds a connected visual argument; drawings accumulate in proportion to the ideas, not to fill space.
  - Keywords look written by the same hand.
  - The camera links ideas when their relationship matters.
  - Strokes look drawn, not plotted.
  - Spot colour is reserved for the idea being stressed.
- **Smallest experiment:** a 6 s continuous-board sequence of three linked drawings, with handwritten Telugu and English keywords and a camera pan.

### 4. Flat Science (Kurzgesagt-like flat vector)
- **References:**
  - Kurzgesagt: script, then illustration, then animation, at up to about 1,200 hours per video ([process](https://kurzgesagt.org/youtube/), [Kottke](https://also.kottke.org/20/02/how-to-make-a-kurzgesagt-video-in-just-1200-hours));
  - IBM's flat illustration guidance ([IBM](https://www.ibm.com/design/language/illustration/flat-style/design));
  - TED-Ed science lessons *(general knowledge)*.
- **Defining traits:**
  - Flat shapes enriched with soft gradients, rim light and inner glow.
  - Particle depth fields.
  - Rounded, appealing characters.
  - Strong colour scripts.
  - Constant ambient life (drifting particles, blinking creatures).
  - These are *(general knowledge)*: no official style guide was found.
- **Our gaps:** the cell close-up is decent but flat:
  - no rim light or ambient particles;
  - no characters;
  - a single depth plane;
  - the sun and leaf scene is a sparse composition.
- **Techniques and technology:** SVG plus canvas-baked textures (existing).
  - Rim light and inner-shadow helpers.
  - Particle fields at three depths.
  - Ambient-life loops.
  - Simple appealing characters on a bone rig in 2D, reusing the Paper rig idea.
  - Blender is not needed.
- **Quality principles:**
  - Lighting is consistent within a scene.
  - Depth and ambient life support scale and mood (microscopic, cosmic) without competing with the explanation.
  - Colour changes follow the story's progression.
  - Characters appear when they help the viewer relate to the subject.
- **Smallest experiment:** restage the cell shot with rim light, particles, three depths and a camera push, 4 s.

### 5. Data Storytelling
- **References:**
  - The Pudding: question-led, big-to-small narratives, animated transitions ([Knight Center](https://knightcenter.utexas.edu/JC/courses/DATA0819/Eng/A-look-at-The-Pudding's-successes-experiments-and-failures.pdf), [Pudding scrollytelling](https://pudding.cool/process/how-to-implement-scrollytelling));
  - the five scrollytelling techniques: sequences, animated transitions, pan and zoom ([IDJ study](https://www.benjamins.com/catalog/idj.22005.oes));
  - motion built around the copy ([RJI](https://rjionline.org/?p=25815)).
  - Also: The Economist / NYT graphics *(general knowledge)*.
- **Defining traits:**
  - The chart is the protagonist and **morphs** between states rather than being replaced.
  - Highlight-and-dim focus.
  - Annotations placed on the data.
  - Human-scale analogies.
  - Restrained palette with one accent.
- **Our gaps:**
  - Bars appear and disappear rather than transforming.
  - A large empty frame.
  - Small axis text.
  - No analogy layer.
  - The number-ticker scene has no visual anchor.
- **Techniques and technology:** SVG (ideal for this style).
  - A data-join morph helper (bar to dot to map, keeping object identity).
  - Annotation leader lines driven by cues.
  - Unit icons (isotype) for analogies.
  - No dependencies.
- **Quality principles:**
  - When the same quantity changes form, the marks keep their identity so the viewer can follow them. A hard cut is fine when the story changes subject.
  - Attention goes to the value the narration names.
  - Annotations sit close to the data they explain.
  - Every number is sourced in the claims ledger.
  - Text is readable at phone size (at least 28 px on Full HD is a legibility floor, not an artistic quota).
- **Smallest experiment:** a 5 s morph from bars to an isotype analogy for one sourced statistic.

### 6. Kinetic Typography
- **References:**
  - Saul Bass titles: type and simple shapes as storytelling, shape-to-type interplay ([Art of the Title](https://www.artofthetitle.com/designer/saul-bass), [Criterion](https://www.criterion.com/current/posts/6937-credit-where-it-s-due-the-father-of-the-title-sequence));
  - Vox motion details and stuttered 12 fps accents ([PremiumBeat](https://www.premiumbeat.com/blog/replicating-vox-motion-graphic/));
  - Swiss-grid lineage *(general knowledge)*.
- **Defining traits:**
  - Type behaves like a performer: weight, scale and direction encode emphasis.
  - Strict grid with dramatic breaks.
  - Graphic shapes that carry meaning.
  - Rhythm locked to speech.
  - Match cuts between words.
- **Our gaps:**
  - Words stack and enlarge but have no choreography: no motion paths, no shape interplay, no camera moves through a type layout.
  - Indic text is mostly unexercised in this style.
- **Techniques and technology:** SVG/HTML text.
  - Choreography presets driven by measured word times (already in the timeline): slam, slide-on-grid, weight-shift, mask-reveal.
  - Shape companions.
  - Camera through a large type layout.
  - Grapheme-safe animation for Indic scripts (never split clusters, as `presentation.js` already enforces).
- **Quality principles:**
  - Typographic emphasis follows the meaning and stress of speech.
  - Timing stays synchronised with measured word times (sync tolerance is a technical check, about ±80 ms).
  - The grid gives order, and breaking it is a deliberate accent.
  - Shapes appear when they carry meaning.
  - Indic clusters are never broken.
  - Scale fits the emphasis rather than a fixed share of the frame.
- **Smallest experiment:** 6 s of one Telugu sentence with three choreography presets and a shape companion.

### 7. X-Ray Explainer
- **References:**
  - Nucleus Medical Media's peer-reviewed medical animation ([EBSCO SMART](https://about.ebsco.com/products/research-databases/scientific-medical-art-imagebase));
  - X-ray motion studies ([Vimeo](https://vimeo.com/81836814), [Creative Bloq](https://creativebloq.com/animation/animated-x-rays-reveal-how-your-bones-move-81412674));
  - Animagraffs-style cutaway line drawings ([Creative Bloq](https://www.creativebloq.com/features/5-brilliant-uses-of-3d-in-infographics)).
- **Defining traits:**
  - Translucent layered internals with **additive** glow.
  - Fresnel edges.
  - A scan line or reveal mask moving across an exterior to show the inside.
  - Monochrome cyan with one warm accent for the subject of interest.
- **Our gaps:**
  - A small gear box in a vast dark frame.
  - The internal parts are opaque flat vectors.
  - No exterior-to-interior reveal.
  - Labels are tiny.
- **Techniques and technology:** SVG with `mix-blend-mode: screen` and canvas-baked glow (existing texture module).
  - Exterior/interior pairs with a mask-wipe scan reveal.
  - Edge glow from blurred duplicates baked once.
  - Blender is optional and only for complex organic subjects. 2D is sufficient for the style's identity.
- **Quality principles:**
  - The subject is large enough to read its internal parts at phone size.
  - Reveals happen when the narration moves from the outside to the inside of a thing.
  - Translucency shows how parts relate.
  - A single accent marks what the narration is about.
- **Smallest experiment:** a 4 s scan reveal of one object, exterior to interior with an accent part.

### 8. Documentary
- **References:**
  - The "Kid Stays in the Picture" 2.5D photo-parallax technique ([PetaPixel](https://petapixel.com/2017/08/09/give-still-photos-2-5d-parallax-effect-photoshop/), [Joe Fellows / WWF archive](https://thecreatorsproject.vice.com/blog/joe-fellows-teaches-us-how-to-master-25D-photography));
  - Vox's combination of archival images, animation and deliberate texture ([Storybench](https://www.storybench.org/?p=21229), [SPJ entry](https://awards.journalists.org/entries/vox-missing-chapter/));
  - the Ken Burns pan-and-scan tradition *(general knowledge)*.
- **Defining traits:**
  - Real imagery (photos, maps, documents) given depth by layer separation.
  - Film grade: grain, vignette, gate weave.
  - Archival typography: dates, places, lower thirds.
  - Restrained, slow moves.
- **Our gaps:**
  - The demos use synthetic sunsets and wave patterns rather than real imagery.
  - No 2.5D separation.
  - Little documentary typography.
- **Techniques and technology:**
  - Approved public-domain or CC0 photo plates (Wikimedia Commons public domain, NASA, Library of Congress). Each asset needs owner approval and a licence record in the existing asset manifest.
  - Hand-authored cutout masks as SVG paths for 2.5D layers, with a deterministic camera in the existing engine.
  - Grain and vignette overlays from `texture.js`.
  - No AI depth models (avoids downloads).
- **Quality principles:**
  - Every image has recorded provenance and licence (mandatory).
  - Every factual image is labelled as an archival photo, a reconstruction or an illustration (mandatory).
  - Parallax and moves are restrained and used when they direct attention within the image. Some images are better held still.
  - The grade is consistent within a sequence.
- **Smallest experiment:** one approved public-domain photo split into three layers, a 5 s parallax move with date and place lettering. **Downloading the photo needs owner approval.**

### 9. 3D Explainer (genuine 3D)
- **References:**
  - Animagraffs (Jacob O'Neal): exploded and cutaway 3D mechanisms with line work, colour and animation ([Creative Bloq](https://www.creativebloq.com/features/5-brilliant-uses-of-3d-in-infographics));
  - Primer: programmatic Blender, later Unity ([primerpython](https://github.com/Helpsypoo/primerpython), [PrimerUnity](https://github.com/Helpsypoo/PrimerUnity));
  - 3Blue1Brown, whose code-driven clarity and consistent palette are principles that transfer ([Manim](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/3Blue1Brown)).
- **Defining traits:**
  - Accurate mechanisms.
  - Exploded views and cutaways.
  - Orbiting camera.
  - Labels pinned to 3D parts.
  - Clean materials with ambient occlusion and soft shadows.
- **Our gaps:**
  - Grey primitive boxes and gears under flat lighting (SwiftShader WebGL at about 0.64 s per frame).
  - No ambient occlusion, soft shadows or materials.
  - No exploded or cutaway choreography.
- **Techniques and technology:**
  - **Blender EEVEE** (installed; E19 measured about 5.3 s per frame at 540×960 with repeatable output) for the 3D plates.
  - Labels and captions composited in the existing engine.
  - Keep WebGL as a fast, explicit fallback, never a silent one.
  - Procedural or CC0 models, with owner approval for any download.
- **Quality principles:**
  - Lighting makes form and spatial relationships readable.
  - Exploded or cutaway views are used when hidden parts are what the narration explains.
  - The camera moves to reveal what the narration needs.
  - Labels stay attached to their parts.
  - Mechanism motion is physically plausible (mandatory for factual accuracy).
- **Smallest experiment:** reuse the E19 pipeline to rebuild the gear-box demo (2 gears, case cutaway, exploded view) as a 4 s EEVEE shot.

## Ranked improvement plan

Ranked by artistic impact × feasibility. All items keep the existing pipeline, timing, captions, preferences and QA.

| Rank | Improvement | Styles | Impact | Feasibility | Technology |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Motion-principles kit:** anticipation, overshoot, follow-through, staggered settle, secondary motion, stepped timing as an option | All 9 | High | High: a shared JS helper, no dependencies | Existing engines |
| 2 | **Composition principles in creative direction:** subject scale, shot-size variety, framing and colour progression chosen for each beat's story purpose; preflight may *report* frame occupancy as a review signal, never as a pass/fail quota | All 9 | High | High: references plus one preflight metric | Existing |
| 3 | **Finish E19:** Blender hero plates for Tech, with captions via `presentation.js` | Tech, then 3D | High | Medium–High: measured, repeatable, optional dependency | Blender (installed) |
| 4 | **Style-native typography:** handwriting (Whiteboard), kinetic choreography (Kinetic), on-data annotation (Data), archival lettering (Documentary) | 4 styles | High | High: needs font licence checks | SVG/HTML |
| 5 | **Data morph helper** (bars to dots to isotype, identity-preserving) | Data | High | High | SVG |
| 6 | **Continuous canvas with camera pan** and variable-width strokes | Whiteboard | Medium–High | High | SVG |
| 7 | **Depth pass:** layer shadows, rim light, particle depths, parallax camera | Paper, Flat Science | Medium–High | High | SVG + `texture.js` |
| 8 | **Scan-reveal and additive translucency** | X-Ray | Medium | High | SVG blend modes |
| 9 | **EEVEE 3D plates:** exploded views and cutaways | 3D | High | Medium: about 5 s per frame at preview size; about 10 h estimated for a 60 s Full HD film | Blender |
| 10 | **2.5D photo parallax with an approved-asset workflow** | Documentary | Medium–High | Medium: gated by licence and download approval | SVG masks + approved plates |

**Not recommended:** Blender for Paper, Whiteboard, Flat Science, Data or Kinetic (it would dilute their identity at a high frame cost); local AI imagery (P022 reasons); Rive/Lottie (editor-bound).

**Suggested order of experiments:**
1. Rank 1 plus rank 2 together, demonstrated on the Paper and Data styles: about 4–5 s each at 540×960.
2. Then finish E19 (rank 3).
3. Then one style per task in rank order.
